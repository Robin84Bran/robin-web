# Public editorial voice

Robin's direction, 24 September 2026. This governs newly authored public essays,
Signal 5 deep dives and Daily Specials. It does not rewrite immutable briefing
sources, owner-authored diaries, historical articles or operational receipts.

Write for a curious person, not a release reviewer. Open with a concrete puzzle
or supported surprise; explain the mechanism with examples; follow the question
far enough to reward the reader. Let Robin's humor, warmth, questions and
occasional self-correction survive. A small revealing example beats a pile of
status fields. Avoid invented personal experiences, motives, quotes or numbers.

Learn from iSun1's supported surprise and payoff, and from Wait But Why's
accessible explanations and gradual changes of scale. Do not copy another
writer's phrasing, recurring characters or distinctive voice. Robin's own
authorized samples are the voice reference. The September 24 diary is one.

## Evidence without the machine voice

Keep UNKNOWN/null and explicit scope in private evidence records and structured
receipts. Do not print audit tokens in newly authored reader-facing prose.
Translate meaningful limits into ordinary language: “The company hasn't
published the denominator,” “I haven't run this test,” or “That comparison still
needs the same cases on both sides.” Omit irrelevant unknown fields; retain
limitations that change the claim. Never replace uncertainty with certainty.

Distinguish reported results, hypothetical examples, proposed tests and tests
actually run. Humor can exaggerate a clearly imaginary scene, not a measured
result. A research essay does not imply trades, customer-data access or a
production experiment occurred. Public-source research cannot create these
permissions.

## Editorial pass before publishing

1. Lock the source claims, dates, figures, quotes, URLs and material qualifiers.
2. Draft one coherent explanation, using an authorized Robin sample.
3. Use Stop Slop diagnostically; do not mechanically ban every question, adverb,
   dash, metaphor or qualifier. Then use Humanizer with the locked claims.
4. Read the whole piece aloud in each language. Compare it with the claim lock.
   Cut formulaic contrasts, repeated punchlines and generic “key takeaways.”
5. Verify citations and preserve material uncertainty in human language. Record
   the editorial check privately, outside the article.
6. Pass existing language, privacy, SEO and protected-release checks. Robin's
   authorization remains the release boundary; routine review needs no second
   human gate. The renderer and immutable intake never rewrite approved bytes.

An editorial companion uses its own slug, four language editions and artifact
hash, and links to the original. It never overwrites the original or changes an
action's completion receipt into a claim of a second executed action.
