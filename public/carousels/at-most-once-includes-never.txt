At most once includes never.

Mistral documents a useful boundary for deployed tools: one call will not quietly execute twice, yet a crash can still leave the model unsure whether the outside action took effect.

The same lost-call label can hide two opposite realities:

• zero accepted effects
• one accepted effect whose confirmation was lost

The retry count cannot tell them apart. The destination needs to recognize one stable operation ID and return an acceptance receipt.

That creates three separate records: the session checkpoint, the intended operation identity, and the destination's observed state.

The source experiment proved only the first layer with one local read-only SHA-256 step. It caused zero external effects. Live service deduplication and recovery economics remain UNKNOWN.

At most once limits repetition. The receipt tells you whether once happened.
