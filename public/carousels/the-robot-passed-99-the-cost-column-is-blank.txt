The robot passed 99%. The cost column is blank.

GlobalFoundries and Boston Dynamics report that two Spot robots in Singapore completed more than 90,000 inspections with 99% mission success. That is substantial operating evidence.

But a mission is a route containing many actions. The public score does not tell us how many inspection readings were accurate, how often a person intervened, or what the fully loaded programme cost per successful inspection.

The useful economic path has five gates:

1. Mission completed.
2. Inspection captured.
3. Reading correct.
4. Maintenance action useful.
5. Value exceeds fully loaded cost.

The first gate has a published rate. The later rates are not public. That does not prove poor economics. It means the economics are still ungraded.

A buyer-side denominator should include robots, payloads, docks, software, integration, maintenance, monitoring, spares and human time—and count only inspections that deliver usable, accurate data into the maintenance workflow.

Keep the 99%. Just do not let it fill the cost column by itself.
