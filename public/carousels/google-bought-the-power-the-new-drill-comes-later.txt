Google bought the power. The new drill comes later.

Google has agreed to buy 50 MW from Quaise Energy's Project Obsidian in Oregon. The agreement matters: it gives the project a named buyer and a commercial destination.

But the first 50 MW will use conventional drilling. Quaise plans to use its distinctive millimeter-wave system in a later 200 MW expansion.

That creates four different receipts:

1. The PPA identifies the buyer.
2. Resource work tests the site.
3. The first plant tests delivered power using conventionally drilled wells.
4. The expansion tests whether millimeter-wave drilling can create deeper wells reliably and at an attractive total cost.

The drill also has to be judged as part of a completed well. The US Department of Energy says casing and cement can account for as much as half a geothermal well's cost. Penetration speed is important, but it is not the whole bill.

The contrarian reading is not that Google's agreement is weak. It is that a famous customer can help Quaise reach the test of its famous technology without removing the test.
