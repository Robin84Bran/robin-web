The one-year study does not power the rack.

PJM proposes cutting its grid-study target from two years to one through a new Fast Energization Process. That could be valuable. But the value is easy to misname.

A study can clarify required network work, cost and duration. It can help a weak project withdraw earlier or let a viable project commit with better information. That is option value.

It does not manufacture transformers, issue permits, build substations or commission a data centre.

The useful diligence view keeps three dates separate:

1. Study answer.
2. Conditional or interim service, if available.
3. Full requested service after required facilities are built and accepted.

The proposed process also moves readiness forward. For generation developers, the proposed initial deposit is $10,000 per MW. A 100 MW illustration equals $1 million before later decision-point requirements. Large Load customers have different requirements.

The first completed cycle is expected in 2029. Then the real questions become measurable: withdrawals, early-versus-final cost estimates, study duration, time to interim service, time to full service and capital committed before each milestone.

Keep the one-year target. Just do not let the study clock pretend it powered the rack.
