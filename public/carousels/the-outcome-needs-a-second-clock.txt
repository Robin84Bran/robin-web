Outcome pricing can move software from seats and tokens toward a result. But a result has two clocks.

Clock 1 asks whether the agreed outcome was met. Clock 2 asks whether it stayed resolved through a fixed window.

The economic unit then needs the whole cohort: retained fees minus the variable cost of every attempt, divided by lasting resolutions.

Sierra reports more than $400M ARR and a 64% AOL case-resolution rate. Those company-reported figures establish scale and a named deployment. They do not publish the denominator, reopen window or contribution economics needed for this second clock.

The invoice is one receipt. Time supplies the next.
