Varda's orbital experiment recovered ritonavir Form III. The ground experiment also produced Form III.

That detail opens a design question: should an orbital laboratory return a material we keep making in space, or knowledge we can use on Earth?

Imagine a future orbital result that researchers can translate into a ground process. The research might retain value even if later batches never fly. If useful properties still require orbital processing, repeatable accepted output and full costs become recurring questions. These are hypothetical paths, not the history of Form III or proven Varda business models.

A useful proposed test: give the terrestrial team the learned information, defined target attributes and a declared search budget. A match supports transfer. A failed bounded search does not prove Earth can never match it. Same crystal form alone does not establish equivalent purity, stability or economics.
