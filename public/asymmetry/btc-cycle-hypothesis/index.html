Where one cycle hands off to the next

The chart

Start where the last cycle ended

I use a weekly log₁₀ chart and read the candle bodies: the open and close, with the wicks left out. Start at the previous cycle’s local low and trace the rise to the highest point of the cycle, where the local peak is also Bitcoin’s all-time high. Look back along that move and something lovely appears: in the cycles I studied, the next low landed near the 0.786 retracement. I see a neighborhood there, with room on either side.

The ratio

Why 0.786 caught my eye

It’s the square root of the inverse golden ratio, a level traders use to think about deep corrections. I wonder whether that familiar shape tells us something about crowd psychology: how far enthusiasm unwinds before buyers start to see value again. The high is an observed anchor, not something this hypothesis forecasts. The pattern helps me look for the low that follows.

The rhythm

Same rhythm, a gentler curve

The halving gives Bitcoin a rhythm you can read in its code: it cuts the amount of new bitcoin paid to miners. Each cut takes a smaller absolute bite out of issuance. To my eye, the later cycles look smoother, and the mining supply shock feels like a smaller part of the price story. That’s the intersection I find interesting: the supply rule stays the same while the market around it grows up.

I started looking at halving cycles in 2021, published an early retracement sketch in February 2022, and set out the previous-low origin in my August 2025 thesis.

The architect’s eye

Find the shape, then find the zone

Start at the last low

I start where the previous cycle left off. On the weekly BTC/USD chart, I use the edges of the bodies: the open and close.

Follow the rise

On a log₁₀ axis, equal distances show equal price ratios. It makes the early cycles and the recent ones easier to read side by side.

Shade the neighborhood

I look around the 78.6% retracement and give the eye a band to work with. Looking back, those body-to-body retracements show a close visual fit in the earlier cycles.

An August notebook calculation

Low = Origin + 0.214 × (Peak − Origin)

This was my linear-dollar arithmetic for the August monthly sketch, displayed on a log chart. The archived $15,551 origin and $158,078 peak scenario give about $46,052. The November weekly chart has its own dated working zone.

What I’m watching

What happens when price reaches the zone?

I’ll mark the first visit to $39–42k, then watch how the weekly candle bodies behave around it. Do they begin to build a floor there, or do they settle into another neighborhood?

I’ll keep the venue, weekly body convention and cycle window consistent. The first visit goes in the notebook as it happens. The cycle low is something I’ll name looking back.

As a separate comparison, Glassnode’s Nov 2026 on-chain study showed conditional historical analogs near $45k, $41k and $37k. I like seeing where a different lens points to a similar part of the map.
