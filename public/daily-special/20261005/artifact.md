# Reading the crypto trust-bank lawsuit

Research note · 5 October 2026 · Signal 4

## The question

What would establish that ICBA's challenge has changed the OCC's authority or a particular charter approval? This document maps the requests and the evidence needed to establish an outcome. It does not forecast the litigation or assess a company's suitability as a counterparty.

## Four documents, different jobs

| Document | What it establishes | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICBA complaint, filed 2 October 2026, D.D.C., 1:26-cv-03441 | The plaintiff challenges the final rule, Interpretive Letter 1176 and Protego's approval. The prayer on pages 38–39 asks the court to set them aside and bar reliance on the rule and letter for further charters. | A requested remedy is not an issued court order. |
| OCC Bulletin 2026-4, 27 February 2026 | The agency describes the rule as clarifying existing chartering authority; effective date 1 April 2026. | The bulletin predates the lawsuit and is not a case-specific response. |
| National Bank Chartering final rule, 91 FR 9977, 2 March 2026 | The regulatory wording changes from fiduciary activities to trust-company operations and related activities. The OCC discusses non-fiduciary custody and case-by-case licensing review. | The rule is not a judgment resolving ICBA's complaint or a blanket approval of every proposed business. |
| Interpretive Letter 1176, 11 January 2021 | The OCC distinguishes trust-company activities under section 27(a) from fiduciary capacity under section 92a; the former can include non-fiduciary activities. | The letter does not itself prove the current operating authority of a named institution. |

The complaint is the source for the plaintiff's position. The bulletin, rule and letter are sources for the agency's stated interpretation. Read them with those roles intact. [Complaint][1] · [Bulletin][2] · [Rule][3] · [Letter][4]

## Why the distinction matters

The OCC's example of non-fiduciary custody makes the dispute concrete. Safekeeping and exercising investment discretion on a client's behalf can involve different legal capacities. The contested question is how far trust-company chartering authority reaches. Calling an entity a bank does not settle the permitted activities of its charter or the protections attached to a particular service. [Letter, pages 1–5][4]

My analytical conclusion is that a research record needs separate entries for the general framework, a firm's actual authorization, and any court-ordered change. A story about one entry cannot fill the other two.

## What would change this note

A court order should be recorded by docket, date, remedy, affected agency actions, scope and effective timing, including any stay. An OCC filing would establish the agency's litigation position; a subsequent licensing decision would establish that decision's terms. Neither should be inferred from a news headline.

At the 5 October review, I could not verify a court order disposing of this challenge or a case-specific OCC response. This was a bounded public-source review, not a complete docket certification. It cannot establish that no order exists. The present finding is therefore about the documents and requests, not the lawsuit's outcome.

## Method and limits

Reopened the four primary sources listed below and compared the complaint's requested relief with the agency's published interpretation. Checked recent Specials for duplication and searched public court/agency coverage for later developments. No probability, valuation, capital estimate or operating test was calculated. No legal, investment or counterparty decision follows from completing this research.

The complaint's paragraph 14 cites a 2021 Protego approval, while paragraph 65 describes a February 2026 conditional approval. This note does not resolve that cross-reference discrepancy or certify Protego's present operating status; those require the relevant licensing record and any subsequent orders.
