# The study clock and the delivery clock

Published: 21 November 2026

Research cutoff: 21 November 2026, 09:20 HKT

This bounded research note asks what PJM's proposed Fast Energization Process would actually accelerate, which commitments it would move forward, and why a shorter grid-study target would remain distinct from a project's physical energization date.

## The proposal in one sentence

On 16 November 2026, PJM Interconnection filed proposed tariff revisions with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission in Docket ER27-531-000. PJM proposes studying generation-interconnection requests together with transmission-service requests for eligible and large electricity users through a single 12-month process.

PJM says the process would replace three full System Impact Study phases with an approximately four-month screening phase containing two rounds of planning-level analysis, followed by one System Impact Study. Each screening turn would provide preliminary estimates of the reinforcements, construction duration and costs associated with the portfolio.

The filing remains a proposal. PJM requested a 60-day comment period and Commission action within 120 days. The Commission had not approved the Fast Energization Process by the research cutoff.

## What the 12-month target covers

The target applies to a defined engineering and commercial study process. It does not describe the full interval from a developer's first idea to a working data center, generator, substation or transmission line.

Before the formal application deadline, PJM proposes a Soft Close Date followed by 14 weeks of Early Application Engagement. PJM and the relevant transmission owners would test models, confirm technical inputs and help applicants correct submissions. PJM says only 5% of applications under its current process are correct and complete when first submitted.

Once the formal study begins, the screening phase would supply two early rounds of information. A single full System Impact Study would follow. Negotiation of final service agreements would begin during that later study phase.

The practical promise is earlier information. The physical system still has its own schedule.

## Readiness becomes consequential earlier

The two screening turns lead to Decision Points I and II. Applicants would decide whether to continue and commit readiness funds.

Eligible customers seeking transmission service for a Large Load would need authorization from the relevant transmission owner or electric distributor and an executed Transmission Owner Cost Recovery Agreement to enter the process.

Generation project developers would post the full initial Readiness Deposit with the application. The proposed tariff specifies $10,000 per requested MW. At Decision Point I, $1,500 per MW plus 35% of the developer's share of identified reinforcement costs would be at risk, capped by the initial deposit. At Decision Point II, the greater of the initial $10,000 per MW or 100% of the developer's share of reinforcement and developer-interconnection-facility costs would be required and at risk.

Those deposit figures are proposed terms for generation project developers. They are not a universal cash formula for every data-center customer.

PJM also proposes preventing physical changes to a project or load after the application. A change would require withdrawal and resubmission in the next cycle because the shortened clustered study would not leave time to rebuild individual and portfolio models.

Together, these terms move technical definition, counterparties, agreements and capital readiness toward the front of the process. Earlier answers may reduce time spent waiting for engineering information while making the continue-or-withdraw decision expensive sooner.

## The transition has its own calendar

PJM expects the first Fast Energization submission window to open in late November 2027. Its filing anticipates a Soft Close Date in early May 2028, an application deadline in August 2028 and completion of the first cycle in 2029.

The process cannot begin until Cycle 01 of PJM's current interconnection process is sufficiently advanced. Some tariff sections carry a proposed 16 March 2027 effective date, while the Fast Energization provisions have an indefinite effective date pending that transition.

These dates are PJM's proposed schedule. Actual acceptance, effective dates and first-cycle performance remain to be established.

## Why the second clock remains

A completed grid study can establish what reinforcements are needed, estimate cost and time, allocate responsibilities and define possible service rights. It does not deliver transformers, finish a substation, obtain state permits, construct transmission or generation, complete commissioning or close project financing.

PJM's filing says many projects with signed agreements remain unbuilt or delayed by permitting and supply-chain backlogs. It also describes data centers as buildable in roughly 12–18 months, faster than the generation and transmission facilities needed to serve them.

The proposal includes conditional interim injection and withdrawal rights based on the grid's current configuration. PJM would reassess those rights annually as system conditions change. This could allow some service before every reinforcement is complete, but it is not a promise of full requested service on a fixed date.

## What the first completed cycle could reveal

The proposal is testable through operating evidence. The first cycle can show:

- how many applicants withdraw at each decision point;
- how closely preliminary cost and construction estimates match later requirements;
- whether the formal study finishes near the proposed 12-month target;
- how much time passes between study completion, interim service and full requested service; and
- whether stricter early readiness produces a portfolio containing more projects that reach construction and operation.

Until a cycle runs, realized duration, accuracy, withdrawal rates and delivery outcomes remain unmeasured.

## Research boundary

This research used public sources only. It did not model a named project's financing, contact PJM or a developer, submit an application, allocate capital, change an infrastructure plan or execute any career or investment decision. Completion records a research artifact only.
