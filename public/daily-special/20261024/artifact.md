# The customer arrived before the electricity

Research date: October 24, 2026, Hong Kong time. This artifact places public dates from the Loughton AI-infrastructure project beside Nscale's company-wide commitments and financing dates. It does not allocate company-wide obligations to the site or make an investment decision.

## The bounded question

What does Microsoft's reported withdrawal from Nscale's Loughton agreement reveal when the customer, power, equipment-commitment and financing clocks are placed on one page?

The public record supports a narrow answer: the intended customer window began in Q1 2027, while the reported grid-power horizon moved into the early-to-mid 2030s. Nscale's wider equipment and construction commitments were payable mainly in 2026 and 2027, and at least US$3.1 billion of September 2026 unsecured convertible notes mature on June 15, 2028 unless earlier converted or repurchased. The company has not publicly tied a particular share of those obligations to Loughton.

## Four clocks

| Clock | Public date | What the record supports | What the record does not support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer | Q1 2027 | Nscale said about 23,000 Nvidia GB300 GPUs at an initial 50 MW Loughton facility would support Microsoft Azure from Q1 2027. | The contract, termination rights, deposits, penalties and remedies are not public in the reviewed sources. |
| Power | Early-to-mid 2030s | Reuters and the Guardian reported that Microsoft withdrew after the expected grid timetable moved into the 2030s. | No public date, capacity or cost for an alternative power source was found. |
| Equipment and build commitments | Mainly 2026–2027 | Nscale's S-1 reported US$24.0 billion for undelivered technology equipment and US$3.5 billion for data-center construction and support, company-wide. | The filing does not disclose a Loughton allocation or establish whether site-specific GPUs were ordered, paid for, cancellable or reassigned. |
| Financing | June 15, 2028 | The S-1 disclosed at least US$3.1 billion of unsecured convertible notes due on that date unless earlier converted or repurchased. | Maturity is not proof of a liquidity crisis, and the filing does not assign the notes to Loughton. |

These are not four measures of the same thing. The customer and power dates describe one site's delivery problem. The commitment and financing figures describe Nscale as a whole. Putting them together is useful for locating timing pressure, not for inventing a site-level balance sheet.

## The portfolio gap behind the site story

Nscale defines active capacity as online and revenue-producing. Contracted capacity is under development under signed customer contracts. On its August 31 snapshot, the gap between those categories was large:

| Measure | Active | Active plus contracted |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| GPUs | about 25,000 | about 461,000 |
| Data-center sites | 5 | 17 total: 5 active and 12 contracted |
| Total contract value | US$2.6bn | US$103.4bn |

The comparison does not say that contracted projects will fail. It says the verbs matter. A signed contract is not yet powered capacity; powered capacity is not yet commissioned compute; and only Nscale's active category was described as revenue-producing.

The September 18 filing also predates the reported October 18 exit. Its GPU, site and TCV figures should therefore not be read as post-exit balances.

## What survives the withdrawal

Nscale told the Guardian that it remains committed to Loughton, is exploring alternative power sources and other UK sites, and that its broader relationship and other contracts with Microsoft are unaffected. Its current infrastructure page still describes Loughton as designed to scale to 90 MW.

Those statements preserve possibilities, not a revised delivery schedule. The reviewed sources do not provide the earliest alternative-power date, capacity or cost. Nor do they disclose how the Loughton contract ended or whether any site-specific equipment commitment exists.

That leaves a precise conclusion. AI-compute demand did not disappear; this customer's delivery window and the reported electricity window stopped overlapping. Demand has a shelf life. The credibility of a capacity claim rises only as the project moves from announced, to contracted, to powered, to commissioned, to revenue-producing—and each transition needs its own date.

This is a dated public-source research artifact. It completes the selected research action. It does not trade, spend, allocate capital, change a portfolio, contact anyone, or decide what should happen to Loughton.
