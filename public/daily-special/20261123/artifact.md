# One project, two engineering tests

Published: 23 November 2026

Research cutoff: 23 November 2026, 09:42 HKT

This bounded note asks what Google's 50 MW power-purchase agreement with Quaise Energy establishes about Project Obsidian, and which claims remain for later field and operating evidence. It separates the conventionally drilled first phase from the proposed millimeter-wave expansion.

## The agreement

On 17 November 2026, Quaise announced that Google had signed a power-purchase agreement for 50 megawatts of geothermal power from Project Obsidian in Central Oregon. Quaise says purchases could begin as early as 2030 and would support Google's existing data-center operations in The Dalles and regional communities.

Oregon Public Broadcasting also reported the agreement after speaking with both companies. The reviewed public sources identify the buyer, project and contracted capacity. They do not publish the contract price, duration, conditions, guarantees, termination rights or division of development risk.

A signed PPA supplies stronger market evidence than a general expression of interest: the project has a named buyer for a defined amount of power. Its value to financing and development still depends on the commercial terms and conditions that remain private.

## Phase one uses conventional drilling

Quaise says the initial 50 MW phase will use conventional drilling technology to reach superhot geothermal conditions at commercial scale.

That phase remains a substantial engineering test. The developer must confirm the underground resource, construct wells that survive high temperatures, establish useful fluid circulation, complete a generating plant and deliver electricity. Sustained output would show how the wells, reservoir and plant work together over time.

The first phase can therefore produce commercial evidence for superhot geothermal generation while leaving Quaise's distinctive drilling technology for a separate test.

## Phase two carries the millimeter-wave claim

Quaise's project page describes a 250 MW completed plant developed in phases. After the first 50 MW, the proposed second phase would add 200 MW by reaching deeper, hotter rock below the initial wells, with temperatures up to 850°F, or about 454°C.

Quaise expects that expansion to become the first commercial use of its millimeter-wave drilling system. The hybrid concept uses conventional rotary bits in shallower sedimentary layers. In hard basement rock, a surface gyrotron generates concentrated electromagnetic energy, a metallic waveguide carries it downhole, and the energy melts or ablates rock. Circulating gas carries the fine rock material upward.

The useful measurement extends beyond penetration speed. Commercial evidence would include energy use per metre, equipment availability, maintenance, borehole condition and the fully loaded cost of a completed productive well.

The US Department of Energy explains why this distinction matters. Hard, fractured and abrasive geothermal rock slows conventional drilling; high temperatures limit downhole equipment; and casing and cement can represent as much as half of a geothermal well's cost. A faster way to remove rock addresses one part of the total bill.

## Field and permit status

The Bureau of Land Management's public record covers specified exploration work: surface surveys, one well pad, access work, one resource-confirmation well, storage areas and two freshwater wells on a federal geothermal lease located on US Forest Service-managed land.

BLM issued a categorical exclusion and decision record in September 2025. Its project page describes that decision as an interim approval step and says later notices and drilling permits will follow.

The Oregon Department of Energy's November 2026 biennial report says drilling began during 2026 for a well supporting the proposed 50 MW plant. These records establish active field development and an initial federal decision for the stated exploration scope. They do not establish completion of the generating plant or commercial power delivery.

## What each milestone would establish

- A signed PPA establishes a named buyer and contracted capacity. Its undisclosed terms determine the strength of the commercial support.
- The confirmation well can establish resource characteristics at the site.
- Sustained operation of the 50 MW phase would establish plant output, availability, well behaviour, maintenance needs and delivery performance using conventional drilling.
- The later expansion would test whether millimeter-wave drilling can produce deeper wells reliably and economically in field conditions.

This staging prevents one milestone from borrowing certainty from another. Google's agreement strengthens the first phase's path to market. It does not supply operating data from a completed plant, and the first phase cannot validate drilling technology scheduled for phase two.

## Method and scope

The research reopened the project announcement, project-development page, company drilling explanation, federal project record, federal geothermal technical material, Oregon's 2026 energy report and corroborating local reporting. Claims were kept at the level supported by those dated public sources. Company performance statements were treated as company claims unless government or operating evidence independently established them.

This work used public sources only. It did not contact Quaise, Google, regulators or investors; purchase electricity; invest; trade; apply; change a project; allocate capital; or execute any underlying business or investment decision. Completion records a research artifact only.
