A room can gain money while most of its occupants head for the exit. One person arrives with a large suitcase; several leave with smaller ones. The cash balance rises. The room gets quieter.

That is an imaginary room. But it is a useful way to catch a very real reading mistake: a positive total does not count the people who agree.

## A plus sign with two stories inside

For the five US sessions from September 28 through October 2, 2026, the captured Farside Bitcoin fund table adds to **+$241.1 million**. Split that total once: IBIT contributed +$450.2 million; the other eleven funds combined contributed −$209.1 million. Four funds were positive for the week, five negative and three zero. These are calculations from the provider's table, not independently audited issuer figures. [Data][1] · [Reconciliation][2]

There is no arithmetic disagreement. The sum weights dollars. The count gives each fund one place. They answer different questions.

![IBIT plus the other eleven funds equals the positive Bitcoin total; a separate count shows four positive, five negative and three zero funds. Neither measure counts investors.](/binary-stories/the-total-has-no-headcount/hero.webp)

The tempting sentence is “investors came back.” The table supports a narrower sentence about net flow into the products it lists. It does not identify unique buyers, reveal their other positions or explain their motives. Even the fund count is not a headcount: one fund can contain many investors, and one investor can use several funds.

## The suitcase cannot tell you who carried it

Now change the imaginary room. A thousand people each bring one dollar. Or one person brings a thousand dollars. Both versions add $1,000. A total cannot choose between them. Neither scene describes the observed week; they demonstrate information the sum discards.

Nor does subtracting IBIT recreate a world in which IBIT never existed. The −$209.1 million is the recorded remainder. In a different market, people might choose different products. Accounting can separate columns; it cannot rerun their decisions.

The distinction matters when a flow chart becomes a claim about conviction. To investigate broad participation, start by stating what you mean: more funds receiving money, more distinct holders, or a larger share of assets arriving? Each needs a different denominator. A dollar total answers none of those questions by itself.

The [source Special][2] reconciles the week. This story stays with the inference it cannot make. Later incomplete sessions remain outside this calculation; the provider may revise its automatically updated tables. No investor-level record or return forecast is supplied here.

I would keep the green number. It tells us something useful. I would erase the crowd drawn behind it until someone has actually counted.
