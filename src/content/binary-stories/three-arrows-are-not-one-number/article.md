Three down arrows are not one number.

In the latest complete window, estimated flows for U.S.-listed Bitcoin products fell by $1.282 billion. Ether-product flows fell by another $365 million. The estimated dollar value of tracked USD-pegged stablecoins fell by $5.148 billion.

All three gauges pointed down.

The tempting move is to add the losses and announce that roughly $6.8 billion left crypto. That arithmetic is clean. The measurement would be wrong.

Two readings are flows through listed products over five U.S. trading sessions. The third is a change in an estimated stock of stablecoins between two calendar-day endpoints. A pipe meter and a reservoir gauge can agree on direction without measuring the same water.

The reversal is useful: the observation becomes stronger when the numbers remain separate.

## Agreement without addition

Bitcoin and Ether exchange-traded products leave a weekly flow record. Farside’s tables estimate the net value moving through each product group’s creation and redemption mechanism. The SEC has permitted authorized participants to create and redeem crypto ETP shares in kind, so the value moving through that mechanism need not be a simple cash transfer.

Stablecoin circulation answers another question. DefiLlama tracks an estimated dollar value of tokens in circulation and describes stablecoin market capitalization as a proxy for liquidity, trust and adoption. It is a level observed at a point in time, not a weekly ETP flow.

![Two product-flow meters and a separate stablecoin reservoir all point down, while a measurement boundary prevents their values from being added.](/binary-stories/three-arrows-are-not-one-number/hero.webp)

The latest readings were:

| Gauge | Window | Reading |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Bitcoin products | 2–6 November, five sessions | -$1.282bn |
| Ether products | 2–6 November, five sessions | -$0.365bn |
| USD-pegged stablecoins | 1–8 November, endpoint change | -$5.148bn / -1.7253% |

The first two values can be added because they are aligned estimates of weekly product flows. Their combined reading was -$1.647 billion. The stablecoin change cannot be appended to that total without changing the meaning of the result.

What survives is breadth: three differently constructed gauges contracted across adjacent windows.

## A green day inside a red week

Direction also hides path.

Four of the five product sessions were negative. Wednesday was not. On 4 November, Bitcoin products recorded an estimated $74.6 million inflow and Ether products $53.0 million.

That one positive session did not reverse the weekly totals. It does prevent a lazy description of five uninterrupted days of flight. A weekly signal can be clear while the route inside it remains uneven.

The same discipline applies to the stablecoin figure. DefiLlama’s endpoint includes a nominal circulation series and a dollar-valued series. The source research used the dollar-valued series to keep the comparison consistent with the earlier October 31 edition. Swapping series because the numbers look similar would silently change the instrument.

## What three arrows can say

On October 31, the gauges disagreed: Bitcoin-product flows were positive, while Ether-product flows and the stablecoin estimate contracted. In the latest window, all three moved down.

That makes the current observation broader. It does not make it causal.

The figures do not identify the investors behind product redemptions. They do not reveal where redeemed assets went. They do not show the same dollars leaving an ETP, passing through a stablecoin and exiting the market. They do not prove that the selected rule predicts prices or improves returns.

They answer a narrower question well: did the proposed condition—both product groups and the aligned stablecoin measure no longer contracting—clear in this complete window?

No. Every selected gauge still contracted.

That is a dated research result, not an allocation instruction. The next useful evidence is not a larger invented total. It is another complete window, measured the same way, followed by enough repeated observations to test whether the rule has any predictive value.

Three arrows can strengthen a breadth signal.

They still do not become one number.
