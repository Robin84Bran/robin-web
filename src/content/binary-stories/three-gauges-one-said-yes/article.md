A green number can make a weak signal look broad.

Across the five latest fully reported U.S. sessions through October 20, spot-Bitcoin ETP flows added up to **+$211.2 million**. That is a real turn above zero.

Two neighboring gauges did not follow. Spot-Ether ETP flows totaled **-$388.9 million** over the same sessions. The USD value of circulating dollar-pegged stablecoins fell **$2.368 billion**, or **0.77%**, from October 13 to October 20.

The picture was not green. It was **(+,-,-)**.

## Breadth is agreement, not addition

Bitcoin and Ether ETP flows share a unit and a five-session window, so they can be summed. Together they were **-$177.7 million**.

The stablecoin series cannot join that arithmetic. It is a stock estimate measured in billions of dollars on a calendar-day clock. ETP flows are daily creation and redemption estimates around listed products, measured here in millions of dollars across U.S. sessions.

Adding the stock change to fund flows would produce a number with no coherent economic meaning. Yet the three gauges can still answer a useful question: are their directions aligned?

They were not.

That distinction matters. Aggregation asks how much. Confirmation asks whether separate windows onto a system point the same way. When the units differ, agreement is often more honest than a grand total.

## One session carried the Bitcoin result

The positive Bitcoin total was also concentrated in time.

October 19 contributed **+$526.7 million**. Remove that session and the other four Bitcoin sessions sum to **-$315.5 million**. The five-session result is still valid; concentration does not erase it. It changes the question from “Did Bitcoin turn positive?” to “How much evidence sits behind that turn?”

Ether stayed negative on every one of the five sessions. On October 20, Bitcoin was **+$23.2 million**, Ether was **-$26.1 million**, and the combined total was **-$2.9 million**. Even the final session resisted the neat story.

None of this reveals motive. The tables do not tell us whether investors changed conviction, authorized participants adjusted inventory, or money moved from one rail to another. Contribution is visible. Intent is not.

## Three gauges, three clocks

The ETP tables illuminate a narrow primary-market mechanism. The SEC permits authorized participants to create and redeem crypto ETP shares in kind. Those operations sit beside secondary-market trading between investors, not above the entire crypto economy.

The stablecoin series is broader but different. DefiLlama reports the USD value of circulating tokens in its `peggedUSD` category. Minting, burning, bridging, classification changes and small price deviations can move that estimate. It is not a direct counter of dollars waiting to buy assets.

So the three gauges should remain separate:

- Bitcoin ETP flow: a completed five-session fund window;
- Ether ETP flow: the matched completed fund window; and
- USD-stablecoin circulation: a seven-calendar-day stock change aligned to the last complete fund session.

Their disagreement is not a failed equation. It is the result.

## A confirmation gate should be boring

A useful breadth gate does not need to forecast price. It only needs to stop one exciting column from borrowing the credibility of the others.

First, require complete sessions. Farside displayed October 21 headline totals when the research was frozen, but several fund cells still contained dashes. Missing cells were not turned into zeros.

Second, keep each gauge in its own unit. Compare directions without inventing a shared pool of money.

Third, treat three-way agreement as permission to investigate breadth, not permission to trade. Price, basis, liquidity, venue location and the composition of stablecoin supply would still need their own evidence.

The modest conclusion is the useful one. Bitcoin-product flows crossed above zero. The other two gauges did not confirm the move.

Three gauges were asked the same directional question.

Only one said yes.
