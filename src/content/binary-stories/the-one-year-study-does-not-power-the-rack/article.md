PJM has proposed cutting a grid study from two years to one.

The name of the proposal is Fast Energization.

Those two sentences are easy to merge into a third: power will arrive one year sooner. The filing does not establish that.

PJM controls a study process. It does not manufacture transformers, issue state permits, build substations or commission a data centre. A faster study can still create real value. It simply creates a different kind of value from an earlier operating date.

For an investor or developer, the useful question is not, “How fast is the process?” It is, “Which uncertainty disappears sooner, and what commitment becomes due when it does?”

## The first clock prices the decision

PJM coordinates the wholesale electricity market and high-voltage grid across all or parts of 13 US states and the District of Columbia. On 16 November 2026, it filed a proposed Fast Energization Process with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission.

The proposal would study generation interconnections and transmission service for eligible large electricity users together in a single process targeted at 12 months. PJM describes that as a reduction from two years to one.

The study does not deliver electrons. It delivers information: the reinforcements a project may need, their estimated cost and duration, and the service rights that may be available.

That information can change a capital decision before construction changes at all.

An unattractive project can be stopped earlier. A viable one can negotiate equipment, land, financing and customer commitments with a clearer network scope. A site can lose value when the study reveals an expensive reinforcement. Another can gain value when the answer is manageable.

This is option value. The calendar is shorter because a consequential answer arrives earlier, not because every downstream task has been compressed.

## The faster answer asks for earlier conviction

PJM's proposed screening phase would provide two quick analytical turns and two decision points before the System Impact Study. The filing says the screening phase would take about four months, with each analytical turn taking less than 60 days.

At those decision points, a project chooses whether to continue. For generation developers, continuing also puts more money at risk.

The proposed initial Readiness Deposit is $10,000 per megawatt. A hypothetical 100 MW generation project would therefore post $1 million. At Decision Point I, the amount at risk would be $1,500 per megawatt plus 35% of the developer's identified reinforcement-cost share, capped at the initial deposit. At Decision Point II, the amount at risk would become the greater of the initial deposit or the developer's full share of specified reinforcement and interconnection-facility costs.

Those terms are proposed, not yet a record of how projects will behave. Large electricity customers also face their own authorization, agreement and service requirements; the generation-deposit illustration should not be applied to them indiscriminately.

The mechanism is nevertheless clear. Faster information is paired with earlier readiness evidence. The process is not merely removing waiting time. It is moving diligence and commitment towards the front.

That can improve the queue if speculative projects withdraw. It can also expose a weak project sooner. The first completed cycles will show which effect dominates.

## The second clock builds the asset

After the study, the physical clock remains.

It includes generation, transmission upgrades, substations, transformers, permits, equipment deliveries, construction and commissioning. PJM's filing explicitly discusses signed projects that remain unbuilt or delayed by state permitting and supply-chain backlogs.

The proposal also includes conditional interim transmission rights based on the grid's existing configuration. Those rights could allow some service sooner, but they would be project-specific and reassessed as conditions change. Interim service is not the same as full requested service.

This creates three dates worth keeping separate:

1. **Study answer** — the network work and estimated cost become clearer.
2. **Available service** — some conditional or partial right may begin, if the system and agreement allow it.
3. **Full energization** — the required facilities are built, commissioned and accepted for the requested service.

Only the first date is directly targeted by the one-year process.

## A faster no can be economically useful

Infrastructure stories often treat delay as pure waste. Some delay is waste. Some is the time required to discover that a plan does not work.

The economic gain from a faster study may therefore appear as a project that never reaches construction. If a $1 billion data-centre plan learns one year earlier that its preferred site needs a prohibitive network upgrade, the avoided commitment can be valuable even though no rack is powered.

The opposite case matters too. A manageable study result can reduce uncertainty around a site's schedule and cost. That can improve financing conversations and coordination with suppliers. It still does not prove that the final service date moved by the same amount.

The correct measurement is not simply months removed from the study. It is the distribution of consequences:

- withdrawals after each decision point;
- early versus final reinforcement-cost estimates;
- time from application to study completion;
- time from study completion to interim service, if any;
- time from study completion to full requested service; and
- capital committed before each of those milestones.

Without those denominators, “twice as fast” remains a process claim, not a project-return claim.

## The first real test starts later

The proposal was still before FERC on 21 November 2026. PJM requested a 60-day comment period and a Commission order within 120 days.

The proposed transition also prevents an immediate result. PJM expects the first submission window in late November 2027, a Soft Close Date in early May 2028, an application deadline in August 2028 and the first cycle's completion in 2029. The Fast Energization provisions carry an indefinite effective date pending progress in the current process.

That means the headline is ahead of the evidence.

The proposal supplies a plausible mechanism: consolidate studies, improve applications earlier, deliver preliminary information sooner and require readiness sooner. It does not yet supply observed withdrawal rates, cost accuracy, interim-service outcomes or final energization times.

Those are not reasons to dismiss the change. They are the columns that will determine its economic value.

Keep the one-year target.

Just do not let the study clock pretend it powered the rack.
