The missing cells disappeared. The disagreement did not.

That is the useful surprise in the latest common window for U.S. spot-Bitcoin products, U.S. spot-Ether products and dollar-pegged stablecoins.

Farside's October 30 rows still contained dashes in several fund columns when the research was frozen. Those rows were left out. The five latest sessions with numbers in every displayed Bitcoin and Ether fund cell were October 23, 26, 27, 28 and 29.

Once the window was clean, Bitcoin products recorded **+$302.9 million**. Ether products recorded **-$271.3 million**. The matched listed-product total was a small positive: **+$31.6 million**.

It would be easy to stop there. The total was green.

But a total can be complete and still hide the shape of its parts.

## Completeness and confirmation are separate gates

Completeness asks a mechanical question: did the source report every cell required by the rule?

Confirmation asks an economic question: did the independent measures move in the same direction?

The first gate passed. The second did not.

Bitcoin's five-session path was **-137.9, +110.3, +176.0, +137.3 and +17.2 million dollars**. Ether's was **+4.4, -88.6, -60.2, -21.4 and -105.5 million dollars**. Four positive Bitcoin sessions after October 23 outweighed four negative Ether sessions after the same date.

Netting those paths produced +$31.6 million. The arithmetic is valid because both series share a unit and a five-session window. The label is where judgment begins. “Listed-product flows were positive” is true in aggregate. “Crypto-product demand was broad” is not established by the same number.

![A complete five-session window feeds separate Bitcoin, Ether and stablecoin measures into a breadth gate. The cells are complete, but the directions do not agree.](/binary-stories/the-clean-window-refused-to-agree/hero.webp)

## The plus sign came from cancellation

The combined figure kept only **10.4%** of the Bitcoin inflow after the Ether outflow was netted against it. That ratio is simple arithmetic: 31.6 divided by 302.9.

It is not a confidence score. It shows how much of one positive series survived after a different series moved the other way.

This distinction matters whenever an aggregate mixes components that can disagree. A small positive total can describe modest shared strength. It can also describe a large positive component almost cancelled by a large negative one. The headline number cannot tell us which structure produced it unless the parts remain visible.

Here, the parts were visible. Bitcoin and Ether did not confirm each other.

The tables also do not reveal motive. They cannot tell whether an investor changed conviction, an authorized participant adjusted inventory, or capital moved from one product into another. The flows are provider estimates around listed products, not a map of every decision behind them.

## The third gauge used another clock

DefiLlama's historical series adds a second kind of disagreement.

Its estimated USD value for dollar-pegged stablecoins fell from **$303,248,946,001** on October 23 to **$300,124,090,437** on October 29. The change was **-$3,124,855,564**, or **-1.0305%**.

That number must stay outside the ETP sum. Stablecoin circulation is a stock estimate measured at calendar-day endpoints. ETP flows are session-by-session flow estimates. Adding the two would create a grand total with mismatched units, clocks and mechanisms.

Direction can still be compared. Bitcoin product flows were positive. Ether product flows were negative. Dollar-stablecoin circulation contracted. The sign vector was **(+,-,-)**.

The complete window did not produce a complete story of money moving between rails. It produced three bounded observations that refused to align.

## A clean disagreement is useful

Incomplete data leaves two live explanations: perhaps the apparent signal is real, or perhaps missing cells will change it.

A complete common window removes the second excuse. It does not force the first explanation to win.

That is why clean disagreement has value. It narrows the question without pretending to answer a larger one. The five-session data support these statements:

- the required Farside cells were reported for the selected sessions;
- Bitcoin product flows were positive over that window;
- Ether product flows were negative over the same window;
- their compatible sum was slightly positive; and
- the aligned stablecoin stock estimate fell.

They do not establish a broad risk-on regime, a transfer between rails, investor intent or a future price path.

## Keep two axes on the dashboard

A useful diligence screen can keep completeness and agreement on separate axes.

When a window is incomplete, wait or narrow it. Do not turn dashes into zeros.

When a window is complete but the measures disagree, preserve the disagreement. Do not let a net total borrow the credibility of breadth.

When a window is complete and the measures agree, investigate further. Agreement is still evidence to inspect, not an automatic trade or allocation instruction.

This is a small design choice with a large consequence. One status answers whether the data are ready. Another answers what the data say. Combining them makes “complete” sound like “confirmed.”

On October 31, the data were ready.

They said the system was split.
