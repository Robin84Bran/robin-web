🏹 **Robin’s Daily Signal Brief｜November 10, 2026**

Today’s thread: models and robots are shortening the preparation required for complex work, while capital markets keep separating commitments, equipment, operations and cash. The useful comparison is accepted outcomes, human intervention and cash conversion rather than the largest model, fleet or megawatt figure.

## 1. Frontier models and agents｜Fable 5.2 cuts prices while thousand-agent work still needs outcome accounting

Date: November 9, 2026.

**Fact:** Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2. Standard pricing is $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, 20% below Fable 5.1’s $10/$50. Anthropic also reports that roughly 1,000 Fable 5.2 agents formalized Fermat’s Last Theorem in 30 hours; its disclosure identifies one run per model using a purpose-built coordination harness. [Anthropic release](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) · [System-card index](https://www.anthropic.com/system-cards)

**Why Robin should care — interpretation:** Lower token prices make a capable executor easier to test inside RobinOS. One large experiment demonstrates coordination potential. Accepted work, human minutes, retries and verification determine useful productivity.

**One Action:** Compare the current configuration with Fable 5.2 on one frozen RobinOS task, preserving acceptance criteria and recording successful-outcome cost, intervention and failure recovery.

## 2. Physical AI and robotics｜GR00T 2 adapts new tasks from a few demonstrations

Date: November 9, 2026.

**Fact:** NVIDIA released GR00T 2, an open world-action model. NVIDIA reports 40% average zero-shot success across 50 tasks absent from the target robot’s training. Five demonstrations per task, less than three minutes of robot data, and a 30-minute fine-tune on one RTX 5090 raised average success to 95%. These figures come from NVIDIA’s disclosed evaluation setup; production reliability, human intervention and customer economics remain unmeasured. [GR00T 2 research](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/gr00t-2/) · [NVIDIA CoRL release](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/robotics-simulation-advancements-corl/)

**Why Robin should care — interpretation:** Skill transfer from human video and other robot bodies can move the scarce input from extensive task-by-task teleoperation toward reliable acceptance and field adaptation. Houston Lab can test whether a few examples genuinely reduce deployment work.

**One Action:** Choose one repeatable bimanual task, cap training at five demonstrations, and record adaptation time, success rate and human recovery minutes for every failure.
