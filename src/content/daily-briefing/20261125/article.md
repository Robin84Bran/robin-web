**Two numbers worth unpacking today: four cents and 400 ounces.** Four cents is iFood’s average inference cost for an agent run; it is not the cost of an accepted result. Four hundred ounces is the size of a London gold bar whose fractional ownership can now be traded, settled and pledged separately. One case asks us to measure the whole cost. The other shows what a new tradable unit makes possible.

## 1. Frontier agents: iFood reports 25,000 runs. The next number is accepted work.

Date: Nov 24.

**Fact:** In an [OpenAI customer case study](https://openai.com/business/customer-stories/ifood/), iFood reports more than **25,000 completed platform runs** at **$0.04 average inference cost per run**. GPT‑5.6 Luna works asynchronously in isolated environments and returns GitLab merge requests; the internal security platform reports more than twice as many vulnerabilities fixed. The case gives no unattended acceptance rate, and the four cents excludes execution infrastructure, retries across runs and human review of the resulting work.

**Why Robin should care —** This is a field comparison for Honest Agent. A correctly formatted completion report lets the next program proceed; it does not establish that the change works. For an OPC, a cheaper model wins only if it does not hand the verification burden back to you.

**One Action:** Replay a batch of RobinOS maintenance jobs with known acceptance criteria and calculate total cost, including failed reruns, infrastructure and review, divided by independently tested deliveries requiring no rescue from Robin.

## 2. Physical AI: WeRide passes RMB 100 million in quarterly robotaxi revenue; over 90% came from overseas

Date: Nov 20.

**Fact:** [WeRide’s Q3 release](https://weride.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/overseas-revenue-1715-yoy-contributing-nearly-half-total-revenue) reports quarterly robotaxi revenue above **RMB 100 million** for the first time, up **204% year over year**, with over 90% from overseas. Separately, it reports positive unit economics in Guangzhou. It does not break total robotaxi revenue into fares, vehicle sales and partner services or claim that the global fleet is profitable.

**Why Robin should care —** Most commercial revenue came from outside China; the unit-economics milestone came from one Chinese city. Combining them would manufacture a global profitability claim. Neither measure is directly comparable to a US operator’s paid ride count.

**One Action:** Look for one figure comparable across geographies: contribution profit per paid vehicle-hour in a mature city, after remote assistance and fleet operating costs.

## 3. Crypto flows: US spot bitcoin and ether ETFs lost a combined $654.6 million in one session

Date: Nov 23 US session; checked Nov 24.

**Fact:** Farside’s completed [bitcoin table](https://farside.co.uk/BTC/) shows **$569.7 million** of net redemptions, including **$509.3 million from IBIT**. Its [ether table](https://farside.co.uk/ethereum-etf-flow-all-data/) shows **$84.9 million** out. At cutoff, both Nov 24 rows still had dashes in the fund columns; their displayed zero totals were not evidence of zero flow.

**Why Robin should care —** Capital left both regulated exposure channels, and bitcoin redemptions were concentrated in one fund. That is more precise than “whales are selling.” It does not identify where the money went or show that onchain usage declined.

**One Action:** On the next completed row, ask: were bitcoin ETFs still net negative excluding IBIT? That distinguishes a single-product redemption from broader withdrawal.

## 4. Programmable finance: pledge part of a gold bar without taking it out of the vault

Date: Nov 24.

**Fact:** The [World Gold Council](https://www.gold.org/news-and-events/press-releases/world-gold-council-facilitates-first-live-transactions-using-pooled) says HSBC, J.P. Morgan and StoneX traded, settled and pledged Pooled Gold Interests in legally binding wholesale pilot transactions. London 400-ounce bars entered a segregated pool; participants obtained proprietary interests in the metal, and physical withdrawal completed the lifecycle. This was more than synthetic price exposure, but it was not a retail public-chain launch. [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/24/jpmorgan-hsbc-and-stonex-complete-first-digital-gold-trades-in-london) provides market context.

**Why Robin should care —** Imagine a borrower short half a bar’s worth of collateral. It can pledge a divisible interest without cutting or moving an entire bar. The more interesting distribution question is whether another lender will accept that interest outside the original arrangement.

**One Action:** Follow the first lender outside the three pilot firms to accept PGI and disclose its collateral haircut. That discount would say more about the usefulness of the claim than headline turnover.

## 6. AI infrastructure: one optics contract puts finished modules and laser diodes on different buying schedules

Date: Nov 24.

**Fact:** [Applied Optoelectronics](https://investors.ao-inc.com/2026-11-24-AOI-Announces-Multi-Year-Long-Term-Agreement-with-Tier-1-Hyperscale-Datacenter-Customer?asPDF=1) signed a multiyear agreement with an existing hyperscale customer. Minimum commitments include approximately **$1.5 billion of 800G transceivers in 2027** and **$700 million of laser diodes over 2028–29**. These are purchase commitments, not shipments or recognized revenue.

**Why Robin should care —** A finished optical module and the light source inside it need not command the same margin. Buying them on different schedules raises an engineering and capital-allocation question for the Token Factory: who captures integration value, and who funds capacity and manufacturing yield first? The agreement does not settle that.

**One Action:** Add a year-by-year module-versus-laser revenue bridge to the Token Factory model and test it against the first actual shipment and gross-margin disclosures.

## 7. Late-stage private markets: Peak Energy expands its Series B to fund battery deliveries

Date: Nov 24.

**Fact:** [Peak Energy](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/peak-energy-expands-series-b-total-financing-to-157-million-to-accelerate-manufacturing-amid-growing-customer-backlog-302949087.html) expanded its [October Series B led by GM Ventures](https://peakenergy.com/news/latest/peak-raises-108-million-new-capital) to **$132 million of equity plus a $25 million HSBC debt facility**. The $157 million is the updated financing total, not an additional raise. Peak reports over **$2.5 billion in customer agreements** and is funding its 4 GWh Sacramento factory ramp, a data-center pilot and deliveries beginning in 2027. Agreement value is not revenue; plant capacity is not output.

**Why Robin should care —** Passively cooled sodium-ion storage has to prove manufacturing yield, field availability and service cost. Manufacturing absorbs cash before acceptance; delays lengthen that cycle. Valuation, preferences, debt pricing, revenue and an accessible allocation were not disclosed. Strategic acquisition is a plausible exit if deliveries scale.

One Action | WATCH: Wait for the first commercial customer to disclose accepted MWh as a share of contracted MWh before looking for secondary access.

## 8. Public equities and life sciences: Cogent’s approval rests on how long it delayed tumor progression

Date: Nov 24.

**Fact:** The [FDA approved](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cancer-approvals/fda-approves-bezuclastinib-combination-sunitinib-advanced-gastrointestinal-stromal-tumors) bezuclastinib plus sunitinib for adults with advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumors previously treated with imatinib. In a 413-patient randomized trial, median progression-free survival was **16.5 months versus 9.2 months** on sunitinib alone; hazard ratio **0.50 (95% CI 0.39–0.65)**. Overall survival was immature. From the Nov 23 to Nov 24 adjusted closes, [COGT](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cogt/history/) went **$28.11→$33.01 (+17.43%)**, versus [QQQ](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/qqq/history/) **$651.03→$638.10 (−1.99%)**, a **19.42-point spread**.

**Why Robin should care —** This is a clinical endpoint against an active treatment, and approval changes commercial access. There is a company-specific fundamental event behind the divergence. The median difference does not mean patients lived 7.3 months longer, and the evidence does not warrant attributing the success to AI.

**One Action:** Track mature overall survival from PEAK, including how allowed crossover after progression affects its interpretation.
