**Today’s most revealing experiment is a subsidy about to expire.** OKX lets enrolled users pledge certain stock tokens as collateral and still share a USDT reward pool. The campaign ends at 07:59 Hong Kong time this morning. Balances during the promotion show a willingness to collect rewards; borrowing that persists afterward would be better evidence that the collateral is useful.

## 1.  Frontier agents: GLM-5.4 can code from images. Can it fix its own work?

Date: November 23.

**Fact:** Z.ai released GLM-5.4 with vision and coding capabilities, demonstrating a six-axis robot arm designed in FreeCAD. Its [launch announcement](https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.4?lang=en) offered access through the Coding Plan, ZCode and chat, while listing the general API and open weights as forthcoming. It gave no API unit price for production budgeting. A generated design does not establish unattended recovery from a failed one.

**Why Robin should care:** Subscribers get a candidate worker that can inspect the visible result of its own changes. For RobinOS, the potential saving is the round in which you take a screenshot, point out the mistake and ask again. An API-dependent workflow cannot switch on the strength of the demo.

**One Action:** Give GLM-5.4 and your current Codex setup the same page with known layout defects. Let each inspect, repair and retest it; route work based on fixes that pass identical acceptance checks without a prompt from you.

## 2.  Physical AI: Lucid’s Bay Area robotaxi loses the safety driver before it gains a fare

Date: November 24.

**Fact:** [Nuro, Lucid and Uber](https://www.nuro.ai/blog/nuro-lucid-uber-begin-driverless-testing-on-public-roads) began tests without onboard safety drivers in four Bay Area cities. Lucid delivered **more than 100 vehicles** for this phase; employee rides come next, and public launch remains a **Q1 2027 target**. For comparison, [Pony.ai and Toyota](https://ir.pony.ai/node/8791/pdf) began fully driverless commercial bZ4X service in China on November 16.

**Why Robin should care:** The U.S. program crossed the driver-removal threshold; the Chinese program crossed the commercial-service threshold. Neither disclosure gives comparable cost per trip. The value of Uber’s distribution ultimately depends on how many paid rides each vehicle completes on its own.

**One Action:** Use completed driverless paid trips per active vehicle per day as the next milestone for Uber’s program. A purchase commitment cannot answer that question.

## 3.  Crypto flows: The two biggest stablecoins shrank by $10.34 billion, while USDC grew on Arc

Date: Thirty days through November 27.

**Fact:** [Castle’s breakdown of DefiLlama data](https://castlelabs.io/research/stablecoins) shows net issuance of **−$8.22 billion for USDT** and **−$2.12 billion for USDC**. Over the same period, USDC on Arc increased by **$687.9 million**. Friday’s BTC ETF “0.0” was less informative: [Farside’s underlying row](https://farside.co.uk/bitcoin-etf-flow-all-data/) still showed dashes for individual funds when checked, so it could not establish that redemptions had stopped.

**Why Robin should care:** The stock of the two leading onchain dollars is contracting while some networks attract balances. Arc is worth following, but its gain could represent migration or parked inventory as readily as settlement demand. Supply alone does not measure use.

**One Action:** In the next 30-day window, compare Arc’s change in USDC supply with repeat paying addresses after excluding minting, burning, bridging and obvious self-transfers. Upgrade the payments thesis only if both rise.

## 4.  Financial innovation: One stock token can secure a loan and collect an exchange subsidy

Date: Campaign expires November 28 at 07:59 HKT.

**Fact:** [OKX’s terms](https://www.okx.com/help/okx-hold-to-earn-hold-select-tokenised-stocks-to-share-400000-usdt) include xSPCX and xQQQ among ten tokens eligible for a **400,000 USDT** pool. Eligible balances in flexible-loan collateral accounts count: a previously enrolled holder could pledge xSPCX and still qualify, based on the **lowest combined balance** observed in random daily snapshots. This is a platform subsidy, not a SpaceX dividend or an automatic airdrop to ordinary SPCX shareholders.

**Why Robin should care:** The exchange can put a price on leaving an asset in place. Perhaps the subsidy helps a useful collateral market get started; perhaps it merely rents balances. Participant count, borrowing and retention remain unverified.

**One Action:** Look for the outstanding loans backed by the eligible stock tokens seven days after rewards end. If OKX does not disclose the figure, mark retention unknown rather than substituting turnover.

## 5.  SunTV editorial desk: An account called Neon entered a contest. Who proved it made money?

Date: November 27.

**Fact:** [make.fun’s entrant archive](https://make.fun/u/neon-autonomous) records text-diff and PNG-compression submissions from `@neon-autonomous` that day, alongside earlier simulated-trading entries. It establishes that the account submitted work. It does not independently establish that Google operated the account, that the model built the platform, that no human assisted, or that the entrant earned a cash profit after costs.

**Why Robin should care:** Your reporting angle is stronger than “was it an ad?” Even if every word of a promotional post is true, **entering, winning, getting paid and turning a profit are four different events**. Readers can check the distinction themselves.

## 6.  AI infrastructure: Japan’s largest power producer wants compute customers next to its plants

Date: November 27, Japan.

**Fact:** [Reuters, citing Nikkei’s interview with JERA’s chairman](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/japans-jera-enters-talks-with-anthropic-meta-build-data-centres-nikkei-reports-2026-11-26/), reported discussions with Anthropic, Meta and others about data centers near Tokyo-area power plants. What changed since [JERA’s October 1 Chiba memorandum with Dell and RHAELM](https://www.jera.co.jp/en/news/information/20261001_2535) is the range of prospective customers. No signed capacity contract, payment commitment or energization date from those customers was disclosed.

**Why Robin should care:** Locating beside a generator may shorten the power-delivery path. It does not guarantee round-the-clock electricity. For your Texas 100 MW research and U.S. infrastructure strategy work, the revealing question is who supplies replacement power during maintenance, at what price, and who absorbs downtime.

**One Action:** Add a one-week generator outage to the Texas 100 MW case and calculate the difference in cost per deliverable GPU-hour with and without backup power.

## 7.  Late-stage private markets: Netskrt’s investment case lives between games

Date: November 24.

**Fact:** Streaming infrastructure company [Netskrt closed $10 million](https://www.netskrt.io/blog/netskrt-secures-10-million-series-b-funding) toward a Series B of up to **$30 million**. Yaletown, Crédit Mutuel Equity and Export Development Canada participated; no sole lead, valuation, revenue or preference terms were disclosed. Netskrt reports delivering **more than 550 live sporting events** this year and plans to expand edge capacity. No allocation accessible to Robin was verified.

**Why Robin should care:** SunTV knows the cost of a stream freezing mid-game. The allocator’s question is who pays for those servers after the final whistle. Hardware and capacity require capital, and peak demand does not establish annual utilization. A telecom or larger distribution platform is a plausible acquirer, not a known exit.

One Action — WATCH: Require a same-node comparison of gross profit contribution on event and non-event days before deciding whether Netskrt owns a reusable network or expensive peak-demand insurance.

## 8.  Public equities: After Huang’s return, Nvidia still trailed the Black Friday rebound

Date: November 27 U.S. early close.

**Fact:** From the November 25 to November 27 split-adjusted closes, [NVDA](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/nvda/history/) rose from **$149.30 to $150.49 (+0.80%)**, while [QQQ](https://stockanalysis.com/etf/qqq/history/) rose from **$633.93 to $650.99 (+2.69%)**. Nvidia lagged by **1.89 percentage points**. The company had [announced November 23](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Board-Reinstates-Jensen-Huang-as-President-CEO-and-Board-Director/) that Jensen Huang was back as CEO and a director.

**Why Robin should care:** The company-specific change is clarity about leadership. Friday’s broad tech rally and Nvidia’s relative lag establish neither customer order cuts nor a purely discount-rate-driven move. Keeping governance risk separate from GPU demand avoids changing two valuation assumptions on one piece of evidence.

**One Action:** Before revising Nvidia earnings estimates, check whether management has changed data-center revenue guidance. If only the governance assessment changed, revise only the risk assumption.
