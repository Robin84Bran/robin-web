🏹 **Robin’s Daily Signal Brief｜November 11, 2026**

Today’s thread: stronger execution capabilities are becoming available, while acceptance, delivery and financing determine how much value they realize. Carbon’s agent architecture, Fervo’s operating power and CoreWeave’s cash requirements deserve to be read together.

## 1. Frontier models and agents｜Carbon makes model specialization a native product

Date: November 10, 2026.

**Fact:** Google opened Gemini 4 Carbon to paying API users and enterprises at introductory prices of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, rising to $4/$20 after November 30. Google Antigravity’s accompanying `/swarm` preview supports specialized models, isolated workspaces, a shared message board and adversarial verifiers. Google also warns that swarms consume quota faster. Independently reproducible total cost per accepted result and intervention rates remain unavailable. [Google release](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/) · [Antigravity explanation](https://antigravity.google/blog/gemini-4-carbon-in-google-antigravity/)

**Why Robin should care — interpretation:** RobinOS can reserve expensive reasoning for planning, auditing and recovery while lighter models execute. Builders who define acceptance and routing benefit; lower token prices do not automatically create cheaper successful outcomes.

**One Action:** Compare one frozen task under single-agent and specialized-agent configurations, recording total cost per accepted result, human minutes and recovery after failure.

## 2. Physical AI and robotics｜Gemini Robotics 2.5 enters automotive production-line trials

Date: November 10, 2026.

**Fact:** Google DeepMind introduced Gemini Robotics 2.5 and began rolling it out to robotics partners. The company reports 96% success for Apptronik Apollo 2 automotive-kitting proofs of concept on Mercedes-Benz production lines in Germany and Hungary. The figure comes from a partner proof of concept; continuous operating hours, intervention frequency and cost per item remain undisclosed. [Original DeepMind release](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-5-accelerates-real-world-physical-intelligence/)

**Why Robin should care — interpretation:** Houston Lab can begin with bounded picking, sequencing and packing tasks, then measure recovery when objects change. The case shows model-factory integration. One trial cannot establish scaled commercial economics.

**One Action:** Follow the next continuous-shift results to test whether 96% task success translates into lower human-support cost.

## 6. AI infrastructure｜Fervo has 33MW operating while 396MW remains on the delivery path

Date: Disclosed November 9, 2026; first commercial operation occurred September 30, 2026.

**Fact:** Fervo Energy reported that Cape Station’s first 33MW GeoBlock is commercially operating. The second block is grid-synchronized and exporting test energy; the third is in cold commissioning. Its new Google contract covers 396MW for fifteen years on a take-or-pay basis, with first power expected in mid-2028. Expected total cost for the three Phase I blocks remains about $7,000 per kW. Future contracted capacity and present generation belong in separate records. [Fervo results](https://ir.fervoenergy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fervo-energy-reports-third-quarter-2026-results) · [Form 10-Q](https://ir.fervoenergy.com/node/7541/ixbrl-viewer)

**Why Robin should care — interpretation:** This provides a real stage map for AI power: commercial operation, test generation, commissioning and a future power contract are different asset states. Linking drilling, equipment, interconnection and contracted cash distinguishes delivered value from future promises.

**One Action:** Track whether blocks two and three reach commercial operation by January 1, 2027, updating replication time and cost per kW.
