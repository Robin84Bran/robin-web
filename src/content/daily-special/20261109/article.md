# Three Crypto Gauges Turned Down Together

Money reaches the crypto market through several routes. Some investors buy shares on a U.S. stock exchange that give them exposure to Bitcoin or Ether, the asset used on the Ethereum network. Others hold dollar-linked digital tokens called stablecoins, which can move between crypto services and serve as a means of payment or trading.

These routes leave different kinds of records. Exchange-traded products publish information from which analysts estimate money entering and leaving the products. Stablecoin data providers track the estimated value of tokens in circulation. Together, these records offer a partial view of how participation in the market is changing.

During the latest complete week examined on November 9, 2026, three selected measures pointed in the same direction. U.S.-listed Bitcoin products recorded estimated net outflows. Ether products did too. The estimated circulating dollar value of U.S.-dollar-pegged stablecoins also fell.

That agreement marked a change from the previous comparison, published on October 31. Then, Bitcoin-product inflows sat alongside Ether-product outflows and shrinking stablecoin circulation. This time, the contraction extended across all three gauges.

The change matters because a proposed investment check was waiting for these channels to stop contracting before considering a return of capital. Understanding what that check can tell us starts with understanding what each gauge measures.

## Two pipes and a reservoir

A Bitcoin or Ether exchange-traded product lets an investor gain exposure to the underlying asset through shares traded on an exchange. Behind those shares is a process through which authorized participants—firms permitted to deal directly with the product—create or redeem shares.

In July 2025, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission approved *in-kind* creations and redemptions for crypto exchange-traded products. In everyday terms, that allows the underlying crypto assets to be delivered or received through this process. A dollar figure attached to a product flow can therefore describe the value of assets moving through the product’s creation and redemption mechanism.

Farside Investors publishes automatically updated estimates of these daily flows. A positive figure indicates estimated net inflows; a negative figure indicates estimated net outflows. The figures describe activity at the product level. The identities of the ultimate investors, their reasons for acting and the later destination of redeemed assets remain separate questions.

Stablecoins provide a different view. These are digital tokens designed to track a reference asset, such as the U.S. dollar. DefiLlama tracks their estimated circulating value and describes stablecoin market capitalization as a proxy for liquidity, trust and adoption. A proxy is an indirect measure: it helps illuminate a condition through something observable.

The distinction becomes clearer with an analogy. Imagine meters on two pipes and a water-level gauge on a separate reservoir. The pipe meters record how much water flowed in or out during the week. The reservoir gauge records how much water was present at two points in time.

Here, the two pipes represent Bitcoin and Ether products. The reservoir represents stablecoin circulation. Falling readings across all three suggest broader contraction across the measured channels. Each gauge still belongs to its own plumbing. Adding the reservoir’s decline to the pipe flows would mix different measurements, while tracing particular dollars between them would require further evidence.

## A brief lift inside a declining week

The product-flow window covers five U.S. trading sessions, November 2–6. At the research cutoff of November 9 at 09:11 Hong Kong time, every displayed product cell for those dates contained a number in Farside’s Bitcoin and Ether tables.

The week began with estimated net outflows in both groups. On Monday, November 2, Bitcoin products recorded $398.6 million of outflows and Ether products recorded $139.7 million. Tuesday brought further outflows of $115.6 million and $85.3 million, respectively.

Wednesday interrupted that pattern. Bitcoin products attracted an estimated $74.6 million in net inflows; Ether products attracted $53 million. That positive day matters because it shows the path within the week. A weekly decline can contain a recovery along the way.

Outflows resumed on Thursday and Friday. Across those two sessions, Bitcoin products recorded another $842.1 million in estimated net outflows. Ether products recorded $193.2 million.

The full five-session totals were:

| Product group | Estimated weekly net outflow |
| --- | ---: |
| Bitcoin products | $1,281.7 million |
| Ether products | $365.2 million |
| Combined | $1,646.9 million |

Together, the two groups recorded about **$1.647 billion in estimated net outflows**. Wednesday’s inflows softened the weekly decline, while the other four sessions determined its direction.

The stablecoin comparison used nearby calendar-day endpoints: November 1 and November 8. Across that seven-day interval, DefiLlama’s estimated circulating dollar value of tracked U.S.-dollar-pegged stablecoins fell from approximately **$298.385 billion to $293.237 billion**.

That is a decline of about **$5.148 billion, or 1.7253%**.

There is an important detail behind that number. DefiLlama’s endpoint contains both a nominal circulation series and a dollar-valued series. The nominal series declined by roughly 5.163 billion units. This comparison uses the dollar-valued series, consistent with the earlier published method, giving the $5.148 billion decline. Keeping those two series distinct preserves a consistent comparison across weeks.

The product flows and stablecoin estimate also run on different clocks. One covers five stock-market trading sessions; the other compares circulation across seven calendar days. Their agreement supports a statement about direction across adjacent windows.

## What the proposed check actually answers

With that background, the question becomes straightforward: had the proposed conditions for considering a return of capital been satisfied?

The proposed check has two parts. First, weekly flows for both Bitcoin and Ether products should stop contracting. Second, the aligned seven-day stablecoin supply measure should stop contracting.

At the November 9 cutoff, **the proposed check remained pending**. Both product groups had negative weekly flow totals, and the stablecoin dollar-value estimate had declined.

The useful new observation is the breadth of that contraction. In the October 31 comparison, Bitcoin products supplied a positive reading while the other two gauges fell. In the latest window, all three readings pointed down. The proposed check therefore faced contraction across every selected measure.

That conclusion has a precise scope. It describes how the latest complete window compares with a chosen rule. Whether that rule improves investment outcomes is a further research question. Answering it would require evidence across repeated periods, including what happened after the conditions cleared and how those outcomes compared with alternative approaches.

The current figures also leave room for several explanations. Product redemptions can arise from different investor decisions. Stablecoin circulation reflects an estimated stock of tracked tokens, with its own issuance, redemption and valuation dynamics. Establishing a common cause, or following the same capital through these channels, would require additional records.

Source quality adds another reason to keep the conclusion dated and specific. Farside says its automatically generated tables may contain errors or inaccuracies. The research checked the displayed rows and their arithmetic; an independent reconstruction of issuer records would provide a deeper level of verification. DefiLlama’s historical coverage and classifications can change, and its circulation estimate remains an indirect view of market liquidity.

These qualifications preserve the value of the observation. The week produced a clear result within the selected measures: estimated Bitcoin-product outflows, Ether-product outflows and shrinking stablecoin dollar value. The research remained a public-source observation, separate from financial execution.

The next complete window can show whether that agreement persists, reverses or gives way to another mixed picture. For now, the story is a widening contraction across three measured channels—and an open question about how long it will last.
