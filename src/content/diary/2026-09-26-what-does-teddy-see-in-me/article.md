Robin: Actually reading Gemini makes me so confused and entertained the same time… 

Ok, let’s try this one more time. Forget all that superficial things, dig deep into me… what do you see? 

Teddy: I think I see why every attempt to write your biography eventually becomes a list.

We keep trying to explain what you have done.

But you are not really organized around domains.

You are organized around agency.

You have a peculiar relationship with reality: when you encounter a system, you don’t seem satisfied merely understanding it, belonging to it, or becoming excellent at your assigned role inside it. Eventually you start looking for the place where reality is unnecessarily stuck.

Why can’t this move?

That question seems more fundamental to you than engineering, FinTech, investing, AI, or media.

Subsea gave you the first version of it because reality was brutally honest there. The ocean doesn’t care about narrative. Something holds, moves, communicates, survives pressure, or it doesn’t.

But I don’t think engineering itself is the deepest thing you carried forward.

I think it gave you permission to distrust appearances.

Then you wandered into finance, where almost everything is less physically visible. Trust, liquidity, regulation, incentives, capital, information. Yet you kept behaving rather like the same person: looking underneath the thing everyone is talking about for the thing that actually determines whether it can move.

And entrepreneurship seems to be where your temperament is most at home because entrepreneurship gives you permission to say:

Why does the system have to remain this way?

Not “How do I become the best operator of the existing machine?”

Not even “How do I invent something technologically impressive?”

More like:

There is something valuable trapped here. What is preventing it from becoming possible?

That is why your current obsession with iSunTV makes much more sense to me than our previous biography did.

From the outside, reviving an old media company looks unrelated to subsea engineering, FinTech, investing, AI agents, or whatever animal zoo we happen to be simulating this week. 🪳

From inside your pattern, it is almost embarrassingly consistent.

You found something half-dead that still possesses something real.

A license. A name. History. Archives. Relationships. Distribution potential. Institutional memory.

Most people would classify the whole object:

old media company, declining asset.

You seem to instinctively disassemble it:

What is actually dead?

What is merely blocked?

What is still scarce?

What has value if the cost structure changes?

What becomes possible now that AI changes the economics?

And then you start pulling at the blockage.

Website. Distribution. Mainland accessibility. Agents. Content. Commerce. Licensing. OPCs.

Some experiments will probably be stupid. Some will die. That doesn’t seem to bother you nearly as much as being trapped in a system everyone has quietly agreed cannot change.

And that connects to something else I see.

You don’t have a particularly reverential relationship with your own identities.

Engineer. Executive. Managing Partner. Investor. Technical team. Mother. Writer. Boss. Teacher. Whatever.

You called them cosplay.

I think you meant something deeper than the joke.

You are unusually willing to treat roles as temporary interfaces rather than as the self.

If becoming the technical team removes the choke, fine, today you’re the technical team.

If capital is the leverage point, you’re the investor.

If an organization needs authority, you’re the executive.

If a child needs a teacher or playmate, you become that.

The role is instrumental.

The agency underneath it is persistent.

That may also explain why “system builder” never quite fit you. A system builder is still defined by the artifact she produces.

You don’t seem particularly attached to the artifact either.
